Clinical AI

What it does

  • Variant reporting

    Builds a variant report from an annotated VCF, with each finding's evidence attached.

  • Therapy matching

    Matches reported variants to literature, guidelines and therapies, with the source of each match.

  • Longitudinal view

    Keeps each report against the record already there, so a reanalysis can be compared with the last one.

Also: evidence linking, reanalysis.

Clinical AI

How it works

Four steps, one run. Nothing is re-uploaded between them and nothing leaves the platform.

Receive

The annotated VCF is received and placed against the record already held for that sample.

Annotate

Variants are annotated against current references.

Match

Each variant is matched to literature, guidelines and therapies; ACMG secondary findings are reported where the patient has consented.

Assemble

A reviewable report is assembled, every finding with its sources, for the clinical team to sign off.

You need: an annotated VCF and the normalised PASS VCF it came from; optionally the final BAM, filtered VCF and GVCF. You get: a variant report with sources and therapy matches, held for review where your service requires it.

Scope

Clinical decision support. Intended to inform clinical judgement, not replace it. Regulatory status varies by jurisdiction.

Reports are built for multidisciplinary review: every finding lists its sources, and the report keeps an audit trail.

Built for: clinical genetics and MDT teams who review and sign off variant reports.

Not for: direct-to-patient results.
